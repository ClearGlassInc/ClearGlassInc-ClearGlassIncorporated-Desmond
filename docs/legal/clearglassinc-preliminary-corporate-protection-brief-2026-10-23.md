## 1. Executive decision brief

ClearGlassInc’s immediate priority should be to establish an evidence-backed protection baseline—not generate more policies before confirming its actual obligations.

Based on the business activities described in this conversation—software architecture, AI agents, automation, cybersecurity, web operations, and e-commerce—the proposed initial review should concentrate on:

1. Identifying any live incident, claim, demand, or time-sensitive obligation.
2. Confirming corporate identity, signing authority, and representation.
3. Reviewing material customer and vendor commitments.
4. Establishing ownership and permitted use of software, brands, and other intellectual property.
5. Mapping confidential information, personal information, and AI-agent permissions.
6. Preparing a proportionate counsel-led escalation and incident-response process.

These are proposed review areas, not findings that ClearGlassInc has violated a law, breached an agreement, or suffered an incident.

### Immediate leadership decisions

- Designate one accountable company matter owner.
- Confirm whether any urgent legal or security matter exists.
- Approve a limited initial document review and an outside-counsel budget.
- Identify which business activities, contracts, and assets are most commercially important.
- Keep company matters separate from personal founder matters unless counsel confirms the appropriate representation arrangement.

## 4. Initial protection workstreams

### A. Corporate authority and governance

Proposed review:

- Verify the exact legal name, incorporation jurisdiction, and status.
- Identify authorized signers and internal spending or commitment limits.
- Establish where executed agreements and corporate records are maintained.
- Identify related entities and any use of personal accounts for company commitments.
- Clarify which matters belong to the company and which require separate personal advice.

Deliverable: a concise company identity and decision-authority sheet supported by records.

### B. Contracts and commercial commitments

Select the first agreements using commercial importance, unusual commitments, dispute history, and exposure—not document count alone.

Review questions:

- Are scope, acceptance criteria, change requests, and payment terms clear?
- Do ownership and licensing terms match the intended business model?
- Can the company actually meet its service, security, privacy, and support promises?
- Are indemnities, liability provisions, insurance commitments, and termination rights understood?
- Are subcontracting, AI use, and data-processing arrangements addressed where relevant?

Deliverable: a contract issue matrix with proposed wording, reasonable fallbacks, and decisions reserved for leadership and counsel. Enforceability remains jurisdiction- and fact-dependent.

### C. Intellectual property and software supply chain

Create an initial inventory of material codebases, brands, domains, content, datasets, and reusable components.

For each asset, record:

```text
Asset → creator → relevant agreement → assignment or licence →
usage restrictions → supporting evidence → unresolved question
```

Do not assume that repository access, payment to a contractor, or possession of a file establishes ownership.

Deliverable: a material-asset rights register identifying documented rights and unresolved questions for counsel.

### D. AI, privacy, and cybersecurity operations

Proposed operating controls:

- Separate read-only research from externally consequential actions.
- Require approval of the exact recipient, content, attachments, and version before external communications.
- Restrict access to confidential information and personal information by role and purpose.
- Maintain a usable record of important agent actions and failures.
- Review model and vendor arrangements before sending sensitive documents.
- Do not treat a drafted policy or prompt as evidence that a control is implemented.

Legal research outputs should undergo meaningful human verification before consequential reliance. Canadian law-society guidance emphasizes verification of AI-generated work and protection of confidential and personal information.[1]

Deliverable: an agent-permission inventory, data-flow map, and tested high-impact approval checklist.

### E. Incident response and insurance coordination

Prepare a process that identifies:

- Who coordinates technical containment.
- Who preserves relevant evidence.
- Who contacts counsel and the insurer.
- Who assesses contractual and statutory notification obligations.
- Who approves external communications.
- Where decisions, facts, and timestamps are recorded.

If PIPEDA applies, federal guidance requires reporting and notification for qualifying breaches creating a real risk of significant harm, and records of all breaches of security safeguards involving personal information under the organization’s control. The applicability of that regime and any other requirements must be determined for the actual incident.[2]

Deliverable: a short incident playbook and reviewed notice-obligation checklist—not a generic assumption that every incident has the same reporting deadline.
