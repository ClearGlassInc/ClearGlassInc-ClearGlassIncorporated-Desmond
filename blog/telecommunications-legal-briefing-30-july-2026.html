Telecommunications Legal Briefing

Three developments that materially change prior compliance posture for Canadian wireless/Internet providers and UK wholesale broadband markets. Statutory prohibitions remain in force even where procedural deadlines are suspended.

Desmond Otieno OdhiamboClearGlass InsightsLegal & Regulatory DeskReviewed 30 July 2026General information — not legal advice

Executive assessment: Three developments materially change the prior briefing. No new broadly controlling appellate telecom judgment, general privacy enactment, lawful-access expansion or major monetary penalty was identified in the official sources reviewed.

Reader standard: This briefing separates established facts, controlling authority, mandatory obligations, uncertainty, commercial impact and prioritized actions. It is written for carrier compliance, legal and executive teams. Entity-specific conclusions require the provider’s own licences, tariffs and correspondence.

1. Canada — Switching-fee show-cause proceeding

Jurisdiction: Canada · Regulator: CRTC · Proceeding: Telecom Notice of Consultation CRTC 2026-155, amended by 2026-155-1

The CRTC had required Bell Canada, Rogers and TELUS to file submissions by 30 July 2026 concerning disputed device-handling, SIM, eSIM, shipping and setup charges. On 29 July the Commission suspended those filing and intervention deadlines while it considers procedural requests from public-interest groups and TELUS. New dates have not yet been set.

Controlling authority

  • Telecommunications Act, including section 27.04
  • Telecom Regulatory Policy CRTC 2026-43
  • Wireless Code and Internet Code where applicable
  • Any final order issued in the show-cause proceeding
The procedural suspension does not suspend the statutory fee prohibition.

Providers remain prohibited from imposing fees captured by section 27.04 and the CRTC’s implementing policy. The named carriers are not presently required to file on 30 July. They must comply with the replacement deadlines once issued.

Uncertainty

The CRTC has not yet determined whether the disputed fees violate the Act; whether specific optional-product, shipping or replacement charges are permissible; whether refunds, administrative monetary penalties or mandatory orders will follow; or what the revised procedural timetable will be.

Commercial impact: High for Canadian wireless and Internet providers. Revenue models involving assisted activation, SIM replacement, device fulfilment and service changes remain exposed.

Prioritized compliance actions

  1. Remove 30 July from the operative filing calendar.
  2. Monitor for the replacement procedural ruling.
  3. Continue the substantive fee audit without waiting for new deadlines.
  4. Preserve pricing, cost, customer-choice and approval records.
  5. Quantify refund and remediation exposure.
  6. Escalate disputed fee launches to legal and executive review.

2. Canada — Area code 851 for Nova Scotia and Prince Edward Island

Jurisdiction: Canada · Regulator: CRTC · Instrument: Telecom Decision CRTC 2026-190 · Effective: 27 May 2028

The CRTC approved a distributed overlay using area code 851 for the existing 782/902 complex. Existing customer numbers will not change. The decision approves the associated planning and relief-implementation documents.

Affected carriers and telecommunications service providers must implement the approved relief plan, including applicable network, routing, database, testing and communications measures. The decision does not create immediate customer-number changes or eliminate existing ten-digit dialing.

Commercial impact: Medium, rising as the implementation date approaches. Affected systems may include switching and routing platforms, billing systems, fraud controls, emergency-service databases, customer relationship systems, dialling validation, enterprise PBXs and autodiallers.

  1. Obtain the final relief implementation plan.
  2. Add the 27 May 2028 activation date to network roadmaps.
  3. Inventory applications that validate or store area codes.
  4. Test routing, rating, fraud and emergency-service systems.
  5. Coordinate customer and enterprise communications.
  6. Prevent software from treating 851 numbers as invalid.

3. United Kingdom — Openreach commercial-offer consultation

Jurisdiction: United Kingdom · Regulator: Ofcom · Affected operator: Openreach · Consultation deadline: 27 August 2026

Ofcom is provisionally proposing to direct Openreach to withdraw its Incremental New to Openreach Customer Offer. Ofcom is not presently proposing intervention against the other notified FTTP and Ethernet offers. The assessment is being conducted under the regulatory framework established by the Telecoms Access Review 2026–31.

No withdrawal obligation is final yet. Openreach remains subject to its existing SMP conditions and notification requirements. Interested parties must submit consultation responses by 5 p.m. on 27 August 2026 if they wish their evidence considered.

Commercial impact: High for Openreach, alternative network operators, wholesale broadband purchasers and retail ISPs. The disputed offer may affect wholesale migration incentives, customer-acquisition economics, altnet investment, long-term wholesale pricing and competitive foreclosure risk.

  1. Model the commercial effect of withdrawal and non-withdrawal.
  2. Submit evidence before 27 August where materially affected.
  3. Preserve wholesale pricing and customer-migration data.
  4. Review offer dependencies in retail pricing and sales forecasts.
  5. Do not describe Ofcom’s proposal as a final prohibition.

Canada — Broadband Fund payment orders

The CRTC approved July 2026 claim forms for Keewaytinook Okimakanak, Sogetel Mobilité and TELUS projects, directing the Central Fund Administrator to distribute the corresponding approved payments. This is recipient-specific funding administration, not a new universal-service obligation applying to all carriers.

Commercial impact: High for named recipients; low sector-wide. Recipients remain bound by their project-specific statements of work, funding conditions, reporting requirements and audit obligations.

Legal conclusions

  1. The Canadian fee proceeding is paused procedurally, not substantively. The underlying statutory prohibition remains effective.
  2. No violation has yet been finally established against Bell, Rogers or TELUS in that proceeding.
  3. Area code 851 creates a future implementation obligation, not an immediate customer-number change.
  4. Ofcom’s Openreach position is provisional. No withdrawal duty exists unless and until Ofcom issues a final direction.
  5. Broadband Fund payment approval is recipient-specific. It does not create a universal entitlement or sector-wide duty.
  6. No new general lawful-access mandate was verified. Disclosure and interception remain instrument- and jurisdiction-specific.
Maintain a telecommunications legal-control register covering jurisdiction, regulator, controlling authority, binding status, effective date, affected systems, mandatory obligation, deadline, evidence required, uncertainty, commercial impact and accountable executive.

Immediate next actions

Update the Canadian fee-proceeding calendar to show deadlines suspended. Continue substantive fee-risk remediation. Begin 851 area-code implementation planning. Assess exposure to the Openreach offer consultation. Audit current regulatory calendars for hard deadlines and suspended dates.
