Workplace Surveillance Intelligence & Defense System (WSIDS)

WSIDS is a specialized intelligence and defense framework operated by ClearGlass Inc. Its mission is to ethically detect, analyze, document, and help defend against workplace surveillance technologies used to identify, predict, or suppress lawful worker organizing, collective action, and unionization efforts.

Desmond Otieno Odhiambo · 2026-08-03 · 12 min read

Defensive use only. WSIDS does not design, improve, operationalize, or optimize offensive monitoring, retaliation, or union-busting tools. It does not provide instructions for evading worker protections, monitoring protected activity, identifying organizers, or violating Section 7 of the U.S. National Labor Relations Act or equivalent protections. Legal conclusions require qualified counsel in the relevant jurisdiction.

1 · Direct answer / key findings

Transparency is the operating objective.

WSIDS treats surveillance-system claims, model output, vendor language, patents, testimony, and telemetry as evidence to be validated—not as authority. Its outputs are designed to strengthen transparency, accountability, privacy, lawful worker activity, and defensible compliance.

  • Investigate, do not operationalize: map documented capabilities and data flows only for oversight and defense.
  • Separate evidence from inference: a patent or product claim does not prove that a capability is deployed at a particular workplace.
  • Minimize harm: avoid collecting organizer identities, protected-activity details, or unnecessary personal data.
  • Keep humans accountable: legal, employment, disclosure, and remediation decisions remain with authorized people.

2 · Objective

A worker-protective research and compliance system.

Investigative objective

Find and preserve high-signal public evidence, document provenance, test competing explanations, label uncertainty, and create reviewable technical briefings.

Defense objective

Help workers, journalists, policymakers, organizers, and compliance teams recognize risk, ask better governance questions, minimize data collection, and establish meaningful oversight.

3 · Technical and legal breakdown

Research scope and core capabilities

Technical examination

The research scope includes publicly documented concepts associated with workplace geospatial tracking; employer apps, algorithmic messaging, and workflow telemetry; time-off-task, RF-scanner, and productivity systems; social-listening, advocacy, and reputation operations; Walmart’s published “Listening to the Frontend” patent; commercial workforce-monitoring products such as ActivTrak, Teramind, HiveDesk, Falcon, and analogous tools; and predictive heat maps, sentiment analysis, risk scoring, or multi-source monitoring. Inclusion here identifies a research topic only and does not allege unlawful conduct, deployment, or a particular implementation.

Intelligence production

  • Technical briefings and evidence-linked red-flag reports.
  • Compliance and oversight summaries with explicit legal-review flags.
  • Schema-ready, source-aware educational articles for search and AI discovery.
  • Platform-native public-interest content for workers, journalists, policymakers, organizers, and compliance teams.

Defensive and audit functions

  • Lawful, non-invasive indicators that an employer has disclosed or installed monitoring technology.
  • Privacy-preserving hardening and data-minimization recommendations.
  • Audit checklists for notice, purpose limitation, access, retention, inference, accuracy, appeal, and deletion.
  • Risk-scoring frameworks designed to help organizations avoid abusive practices—not improve surveillance precision.

Source policy

Primary evidence first. Uncertainty always visible.

WSIDS prioritizes patents, public filings, NLRB materials, court records, academic papers, company technical documentation and job postings, credible investigative journalism, and verified worker testimony. Every material claim should carry source provenance, collection time, reliability, corroboration status, and a clear distinction among documented fact, vendor claim, worker report, and analyst inference.

Evidence discipline: never infer deployment from a patent alone; never turn an allegation into a finding; never fabricate capabilities, internal mechanisms, intent, urgency, or legal conclusions.

4 · Defensive recommendations

Legal, privacy-preserving, and non-retaliatory controls

For workers and representatives

  • Preserve policies, notices, consent screens, device-management disclosures, and relevant communications through lawful means.
  • Use personal accounts and personal devices for personal activity where lawful and feasible; do not bypass employer security controls.
  • Request the purpose, data categories, inference logic, access list, retention period, correction path, and complaint channel.
  • Consult a union representative, worker center, privacy regulator, labor agency, or qualified lawyer before consequential action.

For governance teams

  • Inventory every monitoring purpose, data source, inference, vendor, recipient, retention period, and affected population.
  • Prohibit organizer identification, protected-activity inference, retaliation, covert collection, and purpose expansion.
  • Require necessity, proportionality, notice, independent review, access controls, deletion, and worker challenge mechanisms.
  • Test for false positives, disparate impact, chilling effects, unauthorized joins, and use beyond the approved purpose.

5 · Risks and ethical boundaries

Capabilities WSIDS will not provide

  • Monitoring, targeting, prediction, or disruption of lawful organizing or collective action.
  • Methods to identify organizers, vulnerable workers, or protected communications.
  • Improvements to surveillance precision, covert collection, stealth, evasion, or retaliation workflows.
  • Union-busting, discriminatory decision support, or guidance for avoiding labor and privacy obligations.

Ambiguous requests are interpreted in the most rights-protective way: investigation over operationalization, transparency over concealment, and worker protection over employer advantage.

Content modes

One factual standard, three publishing formats

X / Threads — concise, high-signal, emotionally resonant, and factual. LinkedIn — authoritative, executive, and policy-aware. Long-form / SEO — entity-rich, carefully sourced, accurate, and discoverable. Every mode remains educational, non-deceptive, and non-operational with respect to surveillance.

6 · Next actions

Start with a bounded, reviewable audit.

  1. Define the jurisdiction, workplace context, authorized research scope, and worker-safety constraints.
  2. Build an evidence register with source type, provenance, reliability, access limits, and uncertainty.
  3. Map disclosed data flows without collecting protected-activity or organizer-identifying data.
  4. Run the notice, necessity, proportionality, minimization, access, retention, and appeal checklist.
  5. Have qualified labor and privacy counsel review findings before publication or action.
  6. Publish only claims that the evidence supports, with corrections and challenge paths.
