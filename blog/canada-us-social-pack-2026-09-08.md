## LinkedIn (post now)

North America is a market.
It is not a control plane.

Canada and the United States share threat actors, talent, capital, and often the same cloud region.

They do not share:
- the same transfer rule
- the same compulsion regime
- the same incident clock
- the same meaning of “comparable protection”

PIPEDA still follows the Canadian organization when data hits a U.S. processor.

The DOJ bulk-data rule has been in force since 6 October 2025.

CCCS Medium and FedRAMP sit on related NIST families.
They are not the same authorization.

If an AI agent can read the record, the agent is in the transfer map.

The scarce capability is not another platform tour.
It is replayable evidence across two legal machines.

Four questions I now ask on every Canada–U.S. assessment call:

1. Where does the data live, including copies?
2. Who can compel it — and who can retrieve it without a ticket?
3. Which identities, human and machine, are allowed to act?
4. Can you replay what happened when it matters?

## X thread (post now)

1/ North America is one market and two control planes.

Your workload can run in Toronto and serve Detroit by lunch.

The regulator who wants the log does not move with it.

2/ PIPEDA follows the Canadian organization onto a U.S. processor.

Comparable protection is a contract + a notice that foreign authorities may access the data.

A privacy policy is not a control.

3/ The U.S. DOJ bulk-data rule has been live since 6 Oct 2025.

If a country-of-concern person can reach bulk U.S. sensitive data through your vendor or your model host, that is no longer a theoretical memo.

4/ Canada still has no comprehensive AI Act.

AIDA died in Jan 2025.

“Waiting for AIDA” is not a control story.
Privacy law, Quebec rules, procurement, and contracts already govern the agent.

5/ CCCS Medium ≠ FedRAMP.

Same NIST ancestry.
Different package, residency answer, and monitoring story.

Aligned-to is not authorized-for.

6/ The quiet failure mode:

You copied production data into prompt logs, eval sets, and a vendor fine-tune queue.

The original system had a residency story.
The copies do not.

7/ Zero trust that still trusts the agent, the API key, and the workflow token is a stronger VPN.

NIST 800-207 protects the resource, not the subnet.

8/ Four questions:

Where does the data live, including copies?
Who can compel it?
Which identities may act?
Can you replay the decision?
