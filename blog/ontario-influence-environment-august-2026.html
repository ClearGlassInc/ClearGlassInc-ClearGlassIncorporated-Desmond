Assessment: category discipline comes first

Analytical standard

“Psychological operations” is a formal military concept and is not used here as a catch-all label for political advertising, government communications, media management or misinformation. The defensible question is influence: who communicates, who funds it, what is disclosed, how distribution is targeted, what evidence supports the claim and how much independent scrutiny is possible.

The strongest domestic evidence is financial and documentary: Ontario government advertising reached approximately $111.9 million in fiscal 2024-25, with substantial Protect Ontario-themed spending continuing into 2025-26. The strongest foreign-interference evidence is institutional and official: Canada’s Foreign Influence Transparency and Accountability framework came into force on August 4, 2026, while CSIS public reporting continues to identify several foreign states as active interference and espionage threats.

Threat model: four different operating classes

CLASS 01 · DOMESTIC PUBLIC COMMUNICATIONGovernment advertising and controlled communications

Measure spend, message, targeting, disclosure, timing and access. Do not infer unlawful intent from branding alone.

CLASS 02 · FOREIGN INTERFERENCEClandestine, deceptive, coercive or threatening foreign activity

Use official intelligence, registry data, law-enforcement evidence and attributable investigations. Treat this as a separate threat class.

CLASS 03 · INFORMATION DISORDERSynthetic media, opportunistic falsehoods and engagement-driven narratives

Track provenance and amplification before attributing coordination or state direction.

CLASS 04 · COLLECTION GAPClaims that need evidence before assessment

Closed-event patterns, by-election ad targeting and Ontario-specific enforcement capacity remain collection problems until substantiated.

Domestic messaging: what the evidence supports

Record-scale public advertising

Reporting on the Ontario Auditor General’s advertising review places 2024-25 government advertising at approximately $111.9 million. Separate freedom-of-information-derived reporting places Ministry of Finance advertising at about $21.4 million in 2025-26, with roughly $19.5 million described as Protect Ontario-themed, and Ministry of Energy advertising at approximately $9.93 million.

Brand overlap is measurable; motive is not automatically established

The phrase “Protect Ontario” appears in provincial government advertising and Ontario PC Party political branding. That overlap is an objective transparency issue because public and partisan communications can become harder for audiences to distinguish. It does not by itself establish an illegal campaign or a formal psychological operation.

Operationally useful variables

Media-buy records, creative variants, dates, platforms, geographic targeting, ministry ownership, event-access rules and election-period timing are auditable. The workbench below privileges those variables over speculation about motive.

Foreign interference: a different evidentiary standard

Canada’s Foreign Influence Transparency and Accountability framework entered into force on August 4, 2026. Federal guidance states that new registrable arrangements generally must be filed within 14 days, while qualifying pre-existing arrangements receive the stated transition period.

CSIS’s 2025 public reporting identified the People’s Republic of China, India, Russia, Iran and Pakistan among the main perpetrators of foreign interference and espionage against Canada during that reporting year. Elections Canada has also documented apparent AI-generated videos containing inaccurate election information. These sources justify active monitoring while preserving attribution discipline.

      Collection gaps and guardrails

      • Closed government events: collect invitation records, media attendance, livestream availability, question periods and release timing before calling the pattern systemic.
      • By-election advertising: collect media-buy records, ad-library captures, timestamped screenshots, geotargeting evidence and Elections Ontario rulings or guidance.
      • RCMP Ontario foreign-interference capacity: require an RCMP announcement, budget document, committee testimony or attributable staffing data before asserting expansion.
      • Attribution: do not convert correlation, political alignment, shared slogans or viral repetition into claims of coordinated foreign activity without evidence.
      • Confidence: the 0–100 editorial score is a transparent triage aid based on source authority, corroboration, directness and recency. It is not a statistical probability.
