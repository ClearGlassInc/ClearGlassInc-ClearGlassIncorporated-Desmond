Story Pitch: The Battle Over the [Your City Name] Main Street Bike Lane

1. Describe how you took your topic and turned it into an idea.
I began with the broad topic of local city infrastructure and urban planning. To narrow this down, I reviewed the agenda for the upcoming [Your City Name] City Council meeting. I noticed a highly contested proposal to remove the protected bike lane on Main Street to add an additional lane for cars. I turned this into a specific news story idea by focusing on the human impact: how this decision will affect the safety of daily commuters and the economic vitality of downtown businesses.

2. Why is it newsworthy? How does this relate to current events?
This story is highly newsworthy due to its timeliness, conflict, and impact. The city council is scheduled to vote on this proposal next Tuesday. There is a clear conflict between local cycling advocates and commuters who argue the bike lane causes traffic congestion. It relates to the broader current event of national urban planning trends, where cities are struggling to balance climate change initiatives and alternative transportation against car-centric infrastructure and local business needs.

3. Who is your target audience? Who cares about this issue?
The primary target audience is local residents who commute to work via bicycle or car, as well as the small business owners operating along Main Street. Secondary audiences include urban planning advocates, environmental groups, and city taxpayers who are concerned about how public funds are being allocated for infrastructure projects. These groups care deeply because this decision directly affects their daily commute times, physical safety, and the economic health of the downtown district.

4. Who are your sources? What are they likely to say?

Likely to say: They will argue that removing the bike lane makes their daily commute dangerous, forces them into traffic with cars, and goes against the city's climate goals. They will likely express frustration with the city council for prioritizing car traffic over safety.

Source 2 (Decision Maker): [Name], the City Councilmember who proposed the removal.

Likely to say: They will argue that the bike lane is underutilized and has caused a significant increase in traffic congestion for drivers and delivery trucks. They will likely state that the economic survival of downtown businesses depends on easy car access and parking.

Source 3 (Expert/Advocate): [Name], a representative from a local urban planning organization or a downtown business owner.
