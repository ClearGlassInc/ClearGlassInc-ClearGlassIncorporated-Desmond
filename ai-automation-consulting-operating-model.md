## 6. Payout Method

Revenue should flow through conventional business rails first:

1. **Invoice and contract:** issue from Stripe Invoicing, QuickBooks, Wave, FreshBooks, or another business invoicing system.
2. **Card/ACH processor:** collect card or ACH payments through a reputable processor.
3. **Bank account:** settle revenue into a business checking account for accounting, taxes, and payroll clarity.
4. **PayPal or equivalent:** use only when clients require it; keep records synchronized with accounting.
5. **Crypto wallet:** accept crypto only with a written policy, clear accounting treatment, and jurisdiction-aware tax handling.
6. **Bitcoin:** if native Bitcoin payout is not supported by the payment processor, collect fiat first, reconcile invoices, reserve taxes/operating cash, then convert a defined treasury percentage through a regulated exchange or brokerage account controlled by the business.

The safest operating policy is: client pays fiat invoice, funds settle to bank, bookkeeping is reconciled, taxes and operating reserves are allocated, then an approved treasury policy converts a capped percentage into Bitcoin. Avoid sending client revenue directly to a personal wallet or unmanaged address because it weakens auditability, tax records, and dispute handling.

## 8. Risk Notes

- **Market risk:** buyers may be skeptical of AI hype. Mitigation: sell operational outcomes, quantified ROI, and governed systems.
- **Delivery risk:** integrations and data quality can be worse than expected. Mitigation: paid audit, scoped implementation, assumptions list, and change orders.
- **Client expectation risk:** clients may expect autonomous magic. Mitigation: define approval gates, limitations, test plans, and measurable success criteria.
- **Legal/compliance risk:** data privacy, regulated records, employee monitoring, and automated decisions can create liability. Mitigation: counsel review, DPA, least-privilege access, redaction, audit logs, and human review.
- **Platform dependency risk:** SaaS APIs, LLM models, and automation platforms can change. Mitigation: abstraction layers, model routing, exportable data, versioned workflows, and fallback providers.
- **Scope creep risk:** clients will ask for adjacent automations. Mitigation: written scope, backlog, change request process, and retainer expansion path.

### Data and Ontology

Core ontology entities:

- `Organization`: client, vendor, partner, adversarial entity, agency, or business unit.
- `Person`: operator, analyst, customer, lead, contact, decision-maker, or actor.
- `Workflow`: repeatable business or intelligence process.
- `Event`: inbound signal, alert, message, lead, transaction, report, or anomaly.
- `Case`: human-reviewable unit of work with owner, state, evidence, and decision log.
- `Asset`: system, account, endpoint, domain, repository, dataset, or credential scope.
- `ActionPackage`: recommended action, evidence, risk score, approval status, and execution plan.
- `AutomationRun`: workflow execution record with inputs, outputs, model versions, latency, and cost.
- `FeedbackSignal`: operator rating, correction, rejection reason, outcome label, or post-mission result.

Relationships:

- `Person -> associated_with -> Organization`
- `Event -> mentions -> Entity`
- `Event -> opens -> Case`
- `Case -> contains -> Evidence`
- `Workflow -> produces -> ActionPackage`
- `ActionPackage -> requires_approval_from -> Role`
- `AutomationRun -> used_model_version -> ModelRoute`
- `FeedbackSignal -> improves_candidate -> PromptVersion | WorkflowVersion | RoutingRule`

Every object carries confidence, provenance, temporal validity, classification, compartment, coalition releasability, and lineage. The ontology drives human workflows by defining what analysts see and what actions they can approve. It drives AI behavior by defining which tools can be called, which entities can be retrieved, and which recommendations require human gates.

### AI and Agent Design

Agent types:

- **Analyst Copilot:** answers scoped questions, summarizes cases, explains lineage, drafts reports.
- **Commander Copilot:** produces operational briefings, risk summaries, resource options, and decision packages.
- **Triage Agent:** classifies inbound events, deduplicates signals, opens candidate cases.
- **Enrichment Agent:** queries approved data sources, retrieves context, attaches evidence.
- **Correlation Agent:** links entities across time, geography, workflow state, and historical cases.
- **Recommendation Agent:** drafts action packages with confidence, rationale, alternatives, and risks.
- **Evaluation Agent:** scores outputs against test cases, policy constraints, precision, recall, latency, and operator trust.

No operationally significant action executes without explicit approval. Agents may draft, recommend, summarize, classify, and propose workflow improvements. They may not change objectives, bypass policy, modify production prompts, send external communications, alter records, or trigger high-impact actions without approval.

### Self-Improvement Loop

1. Capture signals: feedback, corrections, query logs, alert outcomes, false positives, false negatives, mission results, latency, cost, and user trust scores.
2. Convert signals into evaluation datasets: labeled cases, expected outputs, policy violations, missed correlations, and accepted recommendations.
3. Generate candidate improvements: prompt changes, routing rules, retrieval filters, threshold adjustments, workflow branch changes, and UI nudges.
4. Test candidates offline against regression suites and red-team suites.
5. Run shadow mode or A/B mode for low-risk tasks.
6. Present change package to human approver with diff, metrics, blast radius, rollback plan, and audit record.
7. Deploy through Apollo-style staged rollout.
8. Monitor drift, precision, recall, latency, cost, operator overrides, and incident rate.
9. Roll back automatically or manually when guardrail metrics fail.

#### Policy check

```python
class PolicyDecision(BaseModel):
    allowed: bool
    reason: str
    required_approval: str | None = None

HIGH_IMPACT_ACTIONS = {"external_notify", "case_close", "task_force_request", "production_prompt_update"}

def authorize_action(user: dict, action: str, entity: dict) -> PolicyDecision:
    if entity["classification"] not in user["clearances"]:
        return PolicyDecision(allowed=False, reason="classification_denied")
    if not set(entity.get("compartments", [])).issubset(set(user.get("compartments", []))):
        return PolicyDecision(allowed=False, reason="compartment_denied")
    if action in HIGH_IMPACT_ACTIONS:
        return PolicyDecision(allowed=False, reason="approval_required", required_approval="mission_commander")
    return PolicyDecision(allowed=True, reason="allowed")
```

#### Workflow state machine

```python
from enum import StrEnum

class CaseState(StrEnum):
    INGESTED = "ingested"
    TRIAGED = "triaged"
    ENRICHED = "enriched"
    CORRELATED = "correlated"
    ACTION_DRAFTED = "action_drafted"
    PENDING_APPROVAL = "pending_approval"
    APPROVED = "approved"
    EXECUTED = "executed"
    REJECTED = "rejected"
    ARCHIVED = "archived"

ALLOWED_TRANSITIONS = {
    CaseState.INGESTED: {CaseState.TRIAGED},
    CaseState.TRIAGED: {CaseState.ENRICHED, CaseState.ARCHIVED},
    CaseState.ENRICHED: {CaseState.CORRELATED},
    CaseState.CORRELATED: {CaseState.ACTION_DRAFTED},
    CaseState.ACTION_DRAFTED: {CaseState.PENDING_APPROVAL},
    CaseState.PENDING_APPROVAL: {CaseState.APPROVED, CaseState.REJECTED},
    CaseState.APPROVED: {CaseState.EXECUTED},
    CaseState.REJECTED: {CaseState.ARCHIVED},
    CaseState.EXECUTED: {CaseState.ARCHIVED},
}

def transition(current: CaseState, proposed: CaseState) -> CaseState:
    if proposed not in ALLOWED_TRANSITIONS[current]:
        raise ValueError(f"illegal transition: {current} -> {proposed}")
    return proposed
```

#### Evaluation pipeline

```python
class EvalCase(BaseModel):
    case_id: str
    input_payload: dict
    expected_labels: dict
    policy_constraints: list[str]

class EvalResult(BaseModel):
    case_id: str
    precision: float
    recall: float
    latency_ms: int
    policy_violations: list[str]
    passed: bool

async def run_candidate_eval(candidate_prompt: str, eval_cases: list[EvalCase]) -> list[EvalResult]:
    results: list[EvalResult] = []
    for case in eval_cases:
        started = monotonic_ms()
        output = await model_router.run(prompt=candidate_prompt, payload=case.input_payload)
        latency = monotonic_ms() - started
        violations = policy_guard.scan(output, case.policy_constraints)
        precision, recall = score_labels(output, case.expected_labels)
        results.append(EvalResult(
            case_id=case.case_id,
            precision=precision,
            recall=recall,
            latency_ms=latency,
            policy_violations=violations,
            passed=precision >= 0.90 and recall >= 0.85 and not violations,
        ))
    return results
```

### Security and Governance

ClearGlassInc Artemis systems use zero-trust execution, least privilege, immutable audit logging, and explicit human approval. Security controls include:

- Need-to-know access control at row, column, entity, case, workflow, and action levels.
- Coalition and compartment boundaries enforced at retrieval and UI layers.
- Data lineage on every record, generated answer, recommendation, and action package.
- Prompt registry with versions, owners, eval results, deployment status, and rollback references.
- Model governance with approved model list, task routing policy, cost ceilings, and prohibited data classes.
- Immutable audit trail for access, retrieval, generation, approval, rejection, execution, and rollback.
- Red-team tests for prompt injection, data exfiltration, unauthorized tool use, and policy bypass.

### Scenario Walkthrough

1. A live event arrives from an approved source and is written to the event bus with source, timestamp, classification, lineage, and confidence.
2. The triage agent classifies it as operationally relevant, deduplicates it against historical events, and opens a case in the ontology.
3. The enrichment agent retrieves related entities, prior incidents, known assets, and recent workflow context using permission-aware search.
4. The correlation agent detects a pattern across time and entities, then attaches a confidence-scored relationship graph.
5. The recommendation agent drafts an action package with evidence, confidence, risks, alternatives, and required approval level.
6. The policy engine marks the recommendation as operationally significant and routes it to a human commander.
7. The operator approves, rejects, or edits the package. The decision and rationale are captured as feedback signals.
8. The system records whether the outcome was useful, accurate, timely, and safe.
9. The evaluation agent converts the result into labeled eval data.
10. A candidate prompt or workflow update is generated, tested offline, and submitted as an improvement proposal.
11. A human reviewer approves the change only if evals improve and policy tests pass.
12. Apollo-style deployment rolls the change to a small ring, monitors trust, precision, recall, latency, and override rates, then expands or rolls back.

The platform gets better because it learns from operator corrections and mission outcomes, but it does not autonomously rewrite its goals, bypass policy, or deploy high-impact changes without human approval.
