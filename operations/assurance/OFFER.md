## Positioning

This is an assurance engagement. It validates controls over AI agents that are
already running in your environment. It is not AI adoption consulting, not an AI
readiness assessment, and not an AI transformation program. Nobody here will
advise you on which model to buy.

The problem this solves is narrower and more urgent. Agents are already
connected to your SaaS tenants, cloud accounts, source repositories, identity
platform, and finance workflows. They hold credentials. They take actions. Most
organizations cannot produce a complete list of them, cannot say who owns each
one, cannot show what each one is permitted to do, cannot reconstruct what any
of them did last Tuesday, and have never tested whether they can stop one.

ClearGlass establishes those facts from evidence, scores the controls by test
rather than by policy document, and hands you a record you can put in front of
an auditor, a regulator, or a board.

## Who buys this

Primary buyers: CIO, CISO, VP IT, Head of Internal Audit, Risk Committee chair.

Market: Ontario mid-market, roughly 100 to 2,000 employees. Large enough to have
real agent sprawl, small enough to have no dedicated AI governance function.

Target sectors and why the pain lands:

| Sector | Why they feel it |
|---|---|
| Financial services and credit unions | Regulator and auditor expectations on system access and change control already exist; agents sit outside the existing control narrative. |
| Healthcare and clinics | Personal health information custody obligations do not pause because an agent, not a person, touched the record. |
| Legal firms | Privilege and client confidentiality break the moment agent data crosses an uncontrolled trust boundary. |
| Municipal government | Public accountability and records obligations mean an unexplainable automated action becomes a public problem. |
| Insurance | Automated decisioning touching claims and underwriting attracts both regulatory and litigation scrutiny. |
| Manufacturing with connected operations | Agent write access reaching operational systems turns an IT exposure into a physical and production one. |

## Tiers

### Tier 1 - Agent Exposure Scan

- **Duration:** 5 business days.
- **Deliverables:**
  - Artifact 1, Agent Inventory Register, complete.
  - Artifact 5, Telemetry and Auditability Assessment, partial. Coverage scoring
    only, no investigation walkthrough.
  - Two-page executive brief naming the top 5 exposures with evidence
    references.
- **Explicitly not included:** permission and tool access mapping (Artifact 3),
  data classification and egress analysis (Artifact 4), control validation
  testing (Artifact 6), remediation roadmap.
- **Purpose:** the entry engagement. Priced to be an easy approval inside a
  discretionary budget.

### Tier 2 - Full Assurance Assessment

- **Duration:** 4 calendar weeks, approximately 18 working days.
- **Deliverables:**
  - All six evidence artifacts, complete.
  - Board-ready executive summary.
  - Prioritized remediation roadmap with effort, owner, and target date per
    item.
- **Purpose:** the flagship engagement. This is the deliverable that answers an
  auditor.

### Tier 3 - Continuous Agent Assurance

- **Duration:** monthly retainer, 6-month minimum term.
- **Prerequisite:** a completed Tier 2 assessment. Tier 3 measures drift against
  a baseline, so without the baseline there is nothing to measure against.
  ClearGlass will not sell Tier 3 standalone.
- **Deliverables:**
  - Monthly re-inventory and drift detection: which agents, permissions, and
    integrations appeared, changed, or disappeared since the last cycle.
  - Quarterly control re-validation, including a live kill-switch test.
  - Quarterly board-facing assurance memo.
- **Explicitly not included:** remediation work, implementation, or system
  configuration. ClearGlass validates; the client or its integrator implements.

## Commercial path

Tier 1 exists to produce the finding that sells Tier 2. A five-day scan that
returns an incomplete inventory and low telemetry coverage scores is not a
failure of the scan, it is the argument for the full assessment. Tier 2 produces
the baseline and the remediation roadmap. Tier 3 turns that one-time baseline
into a standing control, because agent estates drift weekly and an assessment is
stale the month after it ships.

Three follow-on service lines fall out of assessment findings. These are scoped
on findings and are not priced here.

**Governance policy authoring.** Where the assessment finds no agent approval
authority, no offboarding process, or no escalation path, ClearGlass drafts the
policy, the approval matrix, and the operating procedure. Indicative effort: 4
to 8 days. Scoped on findings.

**Remediation implementation oversight.** ClearGlass does not implement. Where
the client's own team or integrator does the remediation work, ClearGlass
reviews the design, witnesses the control test, and signs off that the finding
is closed with evidence. Indicative effort: 3 to 10 days depending on finding
count. Scoped on findings.

**Incident-response readiness exercises.** A tabletop and live-fire exercise
against a named scenario, for example an agent with production write access that
begins taking unexpected actions at 2am on a Saturday. Tests the kill switch,
the escalation path, and the forensic reconstruction. Indicative effort: 3 to 5
days. Scoped on findings.

## What ClearGlass does not do

Stated plainly so scope stays clean and the assurance stays independent.

- **Does not implement or configure client systems.** No changes are made to the
  client environment. Access requested is read-only.
- **Does not sell tooling and does not take vendor referral fees.** No product
  is recommended because someone paid for the placement.
- **Does not perform penetration testing or offensive testing of live systems.**
  Control validation is performed in agreement with the client, on client-run
  test procedures, not by attacking production.
- **Does not provide legal or regulatory opinions.** The assessment flags
  regulatory regimes for the client's counsel to assess applicability. It does
  not tell you whether you are compliant.

This is read-only assessment and advisory work. That constraint is what makes
the resulting record usable as independent evidence.
