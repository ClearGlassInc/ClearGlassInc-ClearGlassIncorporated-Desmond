# ClearGlass AI Dependency & Recovery Review

**Purpose:** Identify critical AI and technology dependencies, expose control gaps, and produce a prioritized recovery plan for leadership.

## Client problem

Organizations often know they use AI, cloud software, APIs, and automation but cannot show how a disruption at a model provider, identity platform, SaaS vendor, cloud region, external API, or regulatory jurisdiction would affect critical operations.

## Core question

**If a model, AI vendor, identity service, cloud provider, API, data source, or jurisdiction changes without warning, which business process fails first—and what evidence proves the organization can recover?**

## Ideal client

- Ontario organizations deploying generative AI, AI agents, workflow automation, or AI-enabled customer/employee tools.
- Critical-minerals, industrial, municipal, professional-services, regulated, and defence-adjacent organizations with low tolerance for operational disruption.
- Leadership teams needing a defensible view of AI-related operational exposure before renewal, procurement, audit, insurer review, or deployment expansion.

## Engagement structure

| Phase | Activity | Client output |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Scope | Identify critical services, processes, owners, and risk tolerance | Review charter and dependency boundary |
| 2. Map | Trace models, vendors, APIs, data flows, identities, tools, integrations, and manual workarounds | AI dependency map |
| 3. Verify | Collect evidence of access control, contracts, logging, resilience, security, and continuity | Evidence register with confidence rating |
| 4. Stress-test | Model realistic disruption scenarios and validate recovery assumptions | Scenario findings and recovery gaps |
| 5. Prioritize | Rank actions by business impact, likelihood, effort, and control value | 30/60/90-day roadmap |
| 6. Brief | Present executive decisions, residual risks, and accountable actions | Leadership decision brief |

## Assessment checklist

### Business criticality

- Which revenue, safety, operational, customer, compliance, and decision workflows depend on AI or automation?
- Who owns each workflow, and what is the maximum tolerable interruption?
- What happens in the first four hours, first business day, and first week after a dependency fails?
- Is there a documented manual fallback, and has it been tested?

### Dependency inventory

- Which models, AI applications, platforms, APIs, plug-ins, agent frameworks, cloud services, identity providers, and data vendors are involved?
- Which dependencies are direct, and which are embedded through suppliers or SaaS products?
- Which services are single points of failure?
- Are model versions, endpoints, API keys, integration owners, and renewal dates documented?

### Data and jurisdiction

- What data enters prompts, retrieval systems, logs, fine-tuning pipelines, integrations, and outputs?
- Is each dataset classified, minimized, and approved for the destination service?
- Where are data and backups stored, processed, retained, and accessed?
- Do contract terms address retention, training use, subprocessors, breach notification, and data return/deletion?

### Identity and control

- Does each agent, integration, and automation use a unique identity?
- Are access scopes least-privilege, time-limited where possible, and reviewed?
- Are secrets centrally stored, rotated, and removed when integrations change?
- Do high-impact actions require human approval?

### Resilience and recovery

- Is there an approved alternative model, vendor, workflow, or manual process?
- Can exported data, prompts, configurations, logs, and documentation be recovered without the primary provider?
- Is there a tested process to revoke credentials, isolate integrations, notify stakeholders, and restore operations?
- Are service commitments, escalation routes, and incident-notification obligations documented?

### Assurance and governance

- Is there an approved AI use policy, risk register, vendor review process, and change-management process?
- Can leadership see material agent permissions, external data flows, incidents, and open control gaps?
- Are technical assumptions separated from verified evidence?
- Has an executive accepted residual risk for critical use cases?

## Deliverables

1. **Executive dependency brief** — material risks, decisions required, and accountability.
2. **AI and technology dependency map** — systems, vendors, data, identities, integration points, and failure concentrations.
3. **Evidence register** — verified controls, missing evidence, assumptions, owners, and confidence ratings.
4. **Disruption scenario pack** — practical failure scenarios and business/process impacts.
5. **30/60/90-day roadmap** — prioritized actions, owners, dependencies, and validation criteria.
6. **Board-ready risk questions** — concise questions leadership can use to test management assurance.

## Completion criteria

The engagement is complete when the client can answer, with evidence:

- Which AI-enabled workflows are business-critical.
- Which vendors, data flows, identities, and tools those workflows depend on.
- What authority agents and integrations possess.
- What fails when a key dependency is unavailable or changed.
- How access is revoked and operations are recovered.
- Which named leaders own each remaining risk and corrective action.
