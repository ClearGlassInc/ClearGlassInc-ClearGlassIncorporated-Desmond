### Discovery Call Structure

1. Confirm business objective and current process.
2. Quantify pain: hours, delay, rework, missed revenue, risk, customer impact.
3. Map systems: CRM, helpdesk, docs, databases, inboxes, Slack, ticketing, dashboards.
4. Identify constraints: compliance, approvals, data access, user adoption.
5. Estimate ROI and urgency.
6. Recommend audit, sprint, or no-fit.
7. Close next step on the call.

### Proposal Framework

- Current state and cost of inaction.
- Target outcome and measurable KPI.
- Scope and non-scope.
- Deliverables and timeline.
- Security and governance model.
- Fixed price and payment schedule.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Retainer path.

### Objection Handling

- **“We can do this internally.”** Position the offer as acceleration, architecture, and delivery discipline, not replacement.
- **“AI is risky.”** Emphasize approval gates, logging, monitoring, rollback, and policy checks.
- **“Too expensive.”** Re-anchor to labor cost, lost revenue, cycle time, and implementation risk.
- **“We tried automation before.”** Diagnose failure mode: unclear ownership, brittle integrations, no monitoring, no change control.

### Close Process

1. Confirm business case.
2. Send proposal within 24 hours.
3. Require 50% upfront for projects or first month upfront for retainers.
4. Use e-signature contract and invoice link.
5. Start onboarding only after payment clears.

## 6. Payout Method

Revenue should flow through compliant business payment rails first:

1. Client signs contract.
2. Invoice is issued through Stripe, QuickBooks, Xero, Wave, or PayPal Business.
3. Client pays by ACH, card, wire, or PayPal.
4. Funds settle to business bank account.
5. Accounting system reconciles invoice, fees, taxes, and payout status.
6. Optional crypto allocation is made after fiat revenue is collected and recorded.

If native Bitcoin payout is not supported, use a conservative fiat-to-Bitcoin process:

- Receive revenue into the business bank account.
- Reserve tax and operating cash first.
- Transfer only approved treasury allocation to a regulated crypto exchange or broker.
- Buy Bitcoin according to a documented treasury policy.
- Move long-term holdings to a controlled wallet with hardware-wallet custody, seed backup, and access policy.
- Record basis, fees, transaction IDs, and wallet addresses for accounting.

Do not request clients to pay Bitcoin unless the contract, invoicing, tax treatment, and custody process are already operationally mature.

## 8. Risk Notes

- **Market risk:** buyers may confuse serious automation with generic AI hype. Counter with ROI math, architecture diagrams, and proof-of-work demos.
- **Delivery risk:** integrations can be brittle. Use scoped pilots, staging environments, retries, monitoring, and rollback.
- **Client expectation risk:** clients may expect autonomous magic. Set explicit human approval gates and acceptance criteria.
- **Legal/compliance risk:** data handling, AI outputs, and security controls must match client obligations. Use written data processing terms and avoid unapproved sensitive data exposure.
- **Platform dependency risk:** APIs, model providers, and SaaS tools change. Abstract providers and maintain fallback paths.
- **Scope creep risk:** productize offers, define non-scope, and charge change orders.
